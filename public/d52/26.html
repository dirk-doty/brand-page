Week 26 · Spring

Fish a new spot

Find a pond or a creek you’ve never fished. They bait the hook and cast the line. You take the picture.

Total time
About 3 hours
Cost
$20 to $60

The morning

You take your kids to a pond or a creek none of you has ever fished. They bait their own hooks and cast. You try a few spots along the bank together, and you take the pictures.

Get ready

Buy your fishing license and check if your kids need one. Most states let kids under 16 fish free. Ask a bait shop for a pond or a creek with public access that you haven’t tried. Buy worms on Friday.

What you need

  • Your fishing license
  • A short push-button rod and reel for each of them
  • Size 6 or 8 hooks
  • Small bobbers, about an inch across
  • Split shot sinkers
  • A tub of nightcrawlers
  • Needle nose pliers
  • Sunglasses or safety glasses for everybody
  • Life jackets for them near deep water
  • Your phone for the pictures

How to do it

  1. Walk the bank first. Look for weeds, a dock or a log in the water, because fish stay close to cover.
  2. Tie a hook on each line. Pinch a split shot six inches above the hook. Clip the bobber two to three feet above that.
  3. They thread a one inch piece of worm onto the hook.
  4. They check that nobody is behind them. Then they cast next to the cover.
  5. They watch the bobber. When it goes under, they lift the rod tip and reel.
  6. If nothing bites in 20 minutes, move down the bank and try again.
  7. Take a picture of them fishing the new spot. If they land a fish, take that picture too.
  8. If they land one, wet your hands and back the hook out with the pliers. They let the fish go, or you keep it if it’s legal.
Keep it safe

Everybody wears glasses, because a hook swings wide on a cast. They look behind them before every cast. They wear life jackets on docks and next to deep water. At a new spot, fish from flat, solid ground and stay away from fast water.

With kids under 6

You cast and they reel it in.

With kids 6 and up

They tie on their own hook and cast for themselves. They pick where you move next.
