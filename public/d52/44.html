Week 44 · Fall

Build the fire pit

Dig the pit and ring it with stone. Light the first fire that night.

Total time
About 4 hours, plus the fire that night
Cost
$100 to $300

The morning

You and your kids dig a fire pit in the backyard and ring it with stone. They dig, haul the gravel and set the blocks. That night they light the first fire in it.

Get ready

Call 811 a few business days ahead so the buried lines get marked. Check your town’s rules on backyard fires. Pick a flat spot at least 10 feet from the house, the fence and any tree, and 25 feet if you have the room. Buy the blocks and the gravel. If you don’t have a yard, help another dad build a pit in his.

What you need

  • A shovel for each of you
  • A tape measure
  • A can of marking paint or a rope
  • A level
  • About 36 fire pit blocks or retaining wall blocks
  • Six bags of gravel
  • A rake
  • A wheelbarrow
  • Gloves
  • Firewood, kindling and matches
  • A bucket of water

How to do it

  1. Mark a circle about three and a half feet across.
  2. They dig out the circle six inches deep and flat on the bottom.
  3. Pour in four inches of gravel and rake it flat.
  4. Set the first ring of blocks around the edge. Check it with the level.
  5. They stack two more rings and stagger the joints.
  6. Clear the grass and leaves for five feet around the pit.
  7. That night, build the fire and let them light it.
  8. Put it out before bed. Pour water on it, stir it and pour again. Don’t leave until the ashes are cool.
Keep it safe

Don’t dig until 811 has marked the yard. Don’t use rocks from a creek or a river, because wet rocks can crack in a fire. Keep a bucket of water next to the pit. Skip the fire on a windy night or during a burn ban.

With kids under 6

They haul gravel with a bucket and pick where the chairs go.

With kids 6 and up

They measure the circle, dig and set every block. They check each ring with the level.
