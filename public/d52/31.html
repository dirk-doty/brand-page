Week 31 · Summer

Fourth of July: rocket launch

Build model rockets together. Launch them and chase them down.

Total time
3 to 4 hours
Cost
$30 to $80

The morning

Each of them builds a model rocket from a beginner kit. You take the rockets to a big open field and launch them one at a time. They run them down when the parachutes open.

Get ready

Buy a beginner rocket kit for each of them, a launch pad with an electric controller, and a pack of A engines. Find an open field at least 100 feet across, with no dry grass and no trees or power lines close by. Check that model rockets are allowed there.

What you need

  • A beginner rocket kit for each of them
  • A launch pad and an electric launch controller
  • A pack of A engines with igniters
  • Recovery wadding
  • Fresh batteries
  • Glue and scissors
  • Safety glasses for everybody
  • A jug of water

How to do it

  1. They build the rockets from the kit instructions. Let the glue dry.
  2. At the field, set the pad in the middle. Clear the leaves and sticks around it.
  3. Pack the wadding and the parachute. Load the engine and the igniter the way the instructions show.
  4. Slide the rocket onto the launch rod. Clip on the controller wires.
  5. Everybody backs up at least 15 feet.
  6. One of them counts down from five out loud and presses the button.
  7. They watch where it comes down and chase it.
  8. If a rocket doesn’t fire, wait a full minute before anybody walks up to it.
Keep it safe

Everybody wears safety glasses at the pad. Launch only with the electric controller. Never light an engine with a match or a lighter. Skip it if the wind is over 20 miles an hour or the grass is dry. Point the rod straight up, and never launch near people, cars or houses. Leave a rocket that lands in a tree or on a power line.

With kids under 6

They press the launch button with you and chase the rocket.

With kids 6 and up

They build their own rocket from the instructions. They pack the wadding and the parachute themselves.
