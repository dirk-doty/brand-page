Week 34 · Summer

Build a raft

Lash a raft together from logs and rope. Put a life jacket on everybody and float the raft in shallow water.

Total time
About 4 hours
Cost
$50 to $200

The morning

You and your kids lash logs together into a raft at the edge of a pond. You slide it into shallow water and see if it floats. Then they climb on and pole it around.

Get ready

Find a pond or a calm lake with a shallow, sandy edge, and get permission to use it. Gather six to eight dry logs, each about six inches thick and eight feet long. Fence posts from the lumber yard work too. Buy the rope.

What you need

  • Six to eight logs or posts
  • Two crosspieces about five feet long
  • 200 feet of half inch rope
  • A saw, for you only
  • A knife for the rope
  • A long pole for pushing
  • A life jacket for each of you
  • Water shoes
  • Gloves

How to do it

  1. Lay the logs side by side at the water’s edge.
  2. Lay a crosspiece over each end.
  3. Lash every log to both crosspieces. Wrap the rope around the log and the crosspiece four times and pull each wrap tight.
  4. They tie off each lashing. You check every knot.
  5. Everybody puts on a life jacket.
  6. Slide the raft into waist deep water and push down on each corner to test it.
  7. They climb on one at a time. You stand in the water next to the raft.
  8. They pole it along the shore.
Keep it safe

Everybody wears a life jacket the whole time. Keep the raft in water you can stand in, close to shore. You stay in the water beside it. Stay off rivers and anything with a current.

With kids under 6

They ride sitting down while you walk the raft through knee deep water.

With kids 6 and up

They wrap and tie every lashing on their own. Then they take turns poling it alone, with you in the water beside it.
