Week 19 · Spring

Fossil hunt

Hunt for fossils in a creek bed. The best find goes on their shelf.

Total time
About 3 hours
Cost
$0 to $40

The morning

You walk a shallow creek and look for fossils in the gravel. They look for shells, stems and leaf prints in the rock. Each of them takes a best find home.

Get ready

Find a place where collecting is allowed. Many state geological surveys list fossil sites, and some states have fossil parks. On private land, ask the owner first. National parks don’t allow any collecting. Go when the creek is low.

What you need

  • Water shoes for everybody
  • A bucket
  • An old toothbrush
  • A magnifying glass
  • Egg cartons for the finds
  • Masking tape and a marker
  • A rock hammer and a chisel
  • Safety glasses for everybody
  • A fossil guide for your state

How to do it

  1. Check the rules for the spot before anybody picks anything up.
  2. Search the gravel bars and the loose rock in the creek bed.
  3. Look for shapes that repeat: ribs, spirals and small round discs with a hole in the middle.
  4. They rinse each find in the creek and scrub it with the toothbrush.
  5. Split flat, layered rock with the hammer and chisel. Leaf prints show up between the layers.
  6. Label every find with the date and the place.
  7. Each of them picks one for their shelf.
Keep it safe

Everybody wears glasses when anybody is hammering. Stay off steep and crumbling banks. Leave if the water starts to rise. On most national forest land you can take common fossils such as shells and plants. Bones and teeth stay where they are.

With kids under 6

They pick from the top of the gravel and you carry the bucket.

With kids 6 and up

They split the layered rock with the hammer and chisel. They look up each find in the fossil guide.
