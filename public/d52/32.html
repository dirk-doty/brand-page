Week 32 · Summer

Start the tree fort

Pick the tree and build the platform. Add to it all summer.

Total time
About 5 hours for the platform and the walls
Cost
$100 to $400

The morning

You and your kids pick the tree and build a low platform with solid walls. They measure, carry the boards and hand them up to you. For the rest of the summer you add to it: a ladder, a roof and whatever they think of next.

Get ready

Pick a healthy hardwood with a trunk at least a foot thick. Print a platform plan made for a single tree, or buy a treehouse bolt kit that comes with one. Buy the lumber and the hardware on the plan. Check your town’s rules if the fort will be near a property line.

What you need

  • The printed plan
  • The lumber and the hardware on the plan
  • A cordless drill
  • A wrench for the bolts
  • A saw, for you only
  • A level
  • A tape measure and a pencil
  • A ladder
  • Safety glasses and gloves for everybody
  • Bags of wood mulch for the ground underneath

How to do it

  1. Pick the height together. Keep the floor four to six feet off the ground.
  2. They measure and mark the boards. You make the cuts.
  3. You set the beams and bolts at the height the plan shows. Use the level.
  4. They pass up the floor boards, and you screw them down.
  5. Build solid walls at least 38 inches high on every open side before anybody plays up there.
  6. Spread the mulch nine inches deep under the fort and six feet out.
  7. They pick what gets built next Saturday.
Keep it safe

Keep the fort low, with the floor no higher than six feet. Nobody goes up until the walls are on. Build away from power lines, and check the bolts and boards at the start of every summer.

With kids under 6

They hand you screws from the ground and help spread the mulch.

With kids 6 and up

They read the plan, measure every board and mark the cuts. They draw up what gets built next Saturday.
