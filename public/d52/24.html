The one match fire

They gather the wood and build the fire. They get one match to light it.

Total time
About 1 1/2 hours
Cost
$0 to $10

The morning

Your kids gather the wood, sort it and build the fire. Then you hand them one match. If it doesn’t catch, they rebuild the fire and try again with one more.

Get ready

Check for burn bans where you live. Use a fire ring, a fire pit or a charcoal grill. If it rained this week, bring dry kindling from home. Fill a bucket with water.

What you need

  • A box of wooden matches
  • A bucket of water
  • A shovel
  • Dry kindling as a backup
  • Marshmallows and roasting sticks

How to do it

  1. They gather three piles. Tinder is dry grass, bark and twigs thinner than a pencil lead. Kindling is pencil thick to thumb thick. Fuel is wrist thick.
  2. They gather a lot of each. A fire burns through kindling fast.
  3. They make a ball of tinder the size of a fist and lean kindling around it in a tepee. They leave a gap on the side the wind comes from.
  4. Hand over one match. They strike it low and light the tinder at the bottom, through the gap.
  5. They add kindling a little at a time, then the fuel.
  6. Roast the marshmallows.
  7. Put it out. Pour water on it, stir it with the shovel and pour again. Hold the back of your hand over the ashes, and don’t leave until they’re cool.
Keep it safe

Tie back long hair. Keep a three foot circle around the fire clear, and nobody runs inside it. The water bucket stays next to the fire the whole time.

With kids under 6

They gather the tinder, and you strike the match together.

With kids 6 and up

They build the fire with no help and get one match. They put it out and check the ashes with you.
