Week 10 · Winter

The teardown

Buy an old radio or an old appliance. Take it apart together, down to the last screw.

Total time
2 to 3 hours
Cost
$0 to $40

The morning

You put something broken on a table and take it apart, one layer at a time. They run the screwdrivers. You name the parts as they come out.

Get ready

Find something broken for free or cheap: a radio, a toaster, a blender, a fan, a printer or a VCR. Skip microwaves, old tube TVs and old radios with glass tubes inside. They can hold a dangerous charge. Leave refrigerators, air conditioners and dehumidifiers alone, so the coolant stays sealed. Skip anything with a big battery. Cut the power cord off.

What you need

  • Phillips and flathead screwdrivers
  • A small bit set with star bits
  • Needle nose pliers
  • Wire cutters
  • Safety glasses and gloves for everybody
  • A muffin tin for the screws
  • Masking tape and a marker
  • An old sheet to cover the table
  • Your phone for photos
  • A flashlight or a toy with big screws for kids under 6

How to do it

  1. Take a photo of it whole.
  2. They take out the outside screws and sort them into the muffin tin.
  3. Lift the cover off. Name what you see: the motor, the circuit board, the gears and the belt.
  4. Keep going one layer at a time. Ask them what they think each part does before you tell them.
  5. Lay every part out in rows. They label each one with the tape and the marker. Take a photo.
  6. Take the pile to an electronics recycling drop.
Keep it safe

Glasses and gloves stay on, because sheet metal edges and springs can cut. You take out any blade yourself. No microwaves, no tube TVs and nothing plugged in.

With kids under 6

Give them a flashlight or a toy with big screws to take apart.

With kids 6 and up

They write every label and take the photos. Ask them how the parts work together.
