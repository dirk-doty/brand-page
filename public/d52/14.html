Week 14 · Spring

Build an owl box

Build a nest box for owls and mount it up high. Check it together every season.

Total time
3 to 4 hours
Cost
$40 to $80

The morning

You build a nest box for screech owls out of one cedar board. They measure, drive the screws and fill it with wood shavings. Then you mount it at least ten feet up.

Get ready

Print a free screech owl nest box plan. Cornell’s NestWatch site has one, and so do many state wildlife agencies. Buy the board and the shavings. Pick a tree or a pole at the edge of some woods or a field. Ask a second adult to come hold the ladder.

What you need

  • The printed plan
  • One 8 foot cedar 1x10, untreated
  • A saw, for you only
  • A drill with a 1/8 inch bit, a 1/4 inch bit and a driver bit
  • A 3 inch hole saw
  • Two clamps
  • A tape measure, a pencil and a square
  • A pound of 1 5/8 inch exterior screws
  • Two hinges and a hook latch
  • A bag of wood shavings
  • Two 3 inch lag screws and a socket wrench
  • Sandpaper
  • An extension ladder
  • Safety glasses for everybody

How to do it

  1. They measure and mark the pieces from the plan: a back, a front, two sides, a roof and a floor. You make the cuts.
  2. Clamp the front piece down. You drill the 3 inch entrance hole where the plan shows it.
  3. They drill four 1/4 inch drain holes in the floor.
  4. Drill a pilot hole for every screw. They screw the box together the way the plan shows. One side goes on hinges so you can clean it out.
  5. They pour in two to three inches of shavings.
  6. Mount it 10 to 30 feet up. You climb, and the other adult holds the ladder. Your kids stand back and tell you when it’s straight.
  7. Pick a spot on the ground where you can all see the hole. Check it from there every season. Owls may take a year or more to move in.
Keep it safe

Glasses stay on. A hole saw can grab the board, so clamp it down and run that tool yourself. Only you go up the ladder, and nobody stands under it. Never touch the birds or the eggs. Federal law protects them.

With kids under 6

They sand the edges, pour the shavings and pick the tree.

With kids 6 and up

They read the plan out loud and check every measurement twice. They pick the spot to watch the box from.
