Week 01 · Winter

Cut your own Christmas tree

They pick the tree and do the sawing. You carry the heavy end.

Total time
3 to 5 hours
Cost
$10 to $90

The morning

You drive to a national forest or a tree farm where you cut your own. They walk around until they agree on a tree. Then they saw it down and you all carry it out.

Get ready

Buy a tree permit for the forest you’ll cut in, on Recreation.gov or at the ranger office. Print the permit and the map. Measure your ceiling. Check the road conditions. Make the hot chocolate and fill the thermos. If there’s no forest near you, find a tree farm that lets you cut your own.

What you need

  • Your permit and the map
  • A bow saw
  • A small folding saw for them
  • Gloves for everybody
  • A tape measure
  • A sled or a tarp to haul the tree
  • Rope or two ratchet straps
  • An old blanket for the car roof
  • A thermos of hot chocolate and cups
Hot chocolate for four
Whole milk4 cups
Cocoa powder1/3 cup
Sugar1/3 cup
SaltA pinch
Vanilla1 teaspoon

How to do it

  1. Stay inside the cutting area on your map.
  2. Let them pick the tree. Measure it against your ceiling height before anybody saws.
  3. Clear the low branches so they can reach the trunk.
  4. They saw close to the ground. You push the trunk away from the cut so the blade doesn’t stick.
  5. Everybody else stands two tree lengths back. Yell “TREE!” when it goes.
  6. Tie the permit tag on. Carry the tree out or pull it on the sled. Dragging it tears the branches.
  7. Tie it to the roof with the trunk facing forward. Then pour the hot chocolate.
Keep it safe

Everybody wears gloves. Keep their free hand away from the blade. On a thick trunk, you make the last cut. Get back to the car before dark.

With kids under 6

They choose the tree and take a few strokes with your hands on the saw.

With kids 6 and up

They read the map and measure the tree against your ceiling height. Then they do most of the sawing.
