Week 08 · Winter

Ice fishing

Measure the ice first. If it’s four inches thick or more, drill a hole and drop a line. Fry what you catch.

Total time
3 to 4 hours, plus 45 minutes to cook
Cost
$60 to $250

The morning

You walk out on a frozen lake, drill holes and fish for panfish. They bait the hooks and pull fish up through the ice. At home you clean the catch and fry it.

Get ready

Read the local ice report, and plan to measure the ice yourself anyway. Buy your fishing license and check if your kids need one. Most states let kids under 16 fish free. Read the size and bag limits. Buy waxworms on Friday.

What you need

  • A hand auger, four to six inches wide
  • A tape measure
  • Ice picks for each of you, worn around the neck
  • A 50 foot rope
  • Short ice rods with light line
  • Small jigs
  • Waxworms
  • Buckets to sit on
  • A sled for the gear
  • Ice cleats
  • Life jackets for them
  • A fillet knife
  • Your fishing license
Fried panfish for four
Panfish fillets4 to 8
Flour1 cup
1 egg and 1/4 cup milk
Salt1 teaspoon
Pepper1/2 teaspoon
Oil1/2 cup
Lemon1

How to do it

  1. Drill a test hole a few steps from shore and measure the ice.
  2. Under four inches of clear ice, go home. White, cloudy ice is half as strong, so you want eight inches of that.
  3. Walk out and test again every 150 feet.
  4. Drill your holes six to ten feet apart.
  5. Put one or two waxworms on a jig. Drop it to the bottom and lift it a foot. Then jiggle it and wait.
  6. When the rod tip dips, they lift the fish up through the hole.
  7. Keep only what’s legal.
  8. At home, fillet the fish. Dip each fillet in egg and milk, then in flour. Fry at 350 degrees for two to three minutes a side.
Keep it safe

No ice is ever 100 percent safe. Stay off anything under four inches, and stay away from inlets, outlets and cracks. Everybody carries ice picks, and they wear life jackets. If you aren’t sure about the ice, fish from the shore.

With kids under 6

Keep it to one hour. Stay on thick ice close to shore, with their life jackets on.

With kids 6 and up

They read the tape with you at every test hole and call out the number. At home, they dip the fillets in the flour.
