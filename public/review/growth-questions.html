1. The results page (20 min, first)

1.1 Ship the v3 results page and relaunch today? v3 adds three cards picked for his lowest section inside the offer, a second full offer box after the stakes, and a sticky buy bar on phones. Our read: ship it after your review, fold in the guarantee and gift calls below, turn ads back on today, and watch the PostHog section markers for a week.

1.2 A named guarantee with a condition? Hormozi: a better guarantee alone can lift conversion 2 to 4x. Draft: "Play one card the night it happens. If it doesn't help, we refund you." Our read: yes, worded through the voice check, kept short as you asked on 13 Nov.

1.3 Name and price the gift on the page? Hormozi wants every bonus named with a real price. Our read: yes for 52 Saturday Mornings, at a price you're comfortable defending.

1.4 Proof before spend. We have no buyer reviews yet, and Hormozi says proof comes before spend. Our read: a personal ask for a review to every buyer from the relaunch, and Dad Shift free to two or three dads you trust in exchange for honest feedback and a review.

2. Paid ads (25 min)

2.1 What do we optimize for when ads come back? On 22 Nov we suggested scorecard finishes. Sam's lead gen lesson changed our read: optimizing for leads teaches Meta to find dads who finish the scorecard cheaply and don't buy, which matches our 23 finishers and 0 orders. Our read: optimize for purchase, with the fixed results page live first. It fits your 20 Nov buyers only rule.

2.2 Broad or layered targeting? Sam and Meta in 2026: broad wins once the creative does the targeting. Your 20 Nov rule: layered. Our read: broad (US, age, Advantage+), with the call out in the creative ("Dads of kids 3 to 14").

2.3 Budget and the kill rule. Hormozi: let a test ad spend up to twice what a buyer pays in his first 30 days, about $58 to $75, then cut it. Our read: $50 a day, that kill rule, and one creative test per Monday logged in one place.

2.4 Creative volume. Hormozi: about 10 bodies and 30 hooks a week. Sam: more creative beats more targeting. Our read: start at 3 bodies and 10 hooks a week, all from the existing photos and the audio cards.

2.5 The scaling trigger. You said spend much more once we hit even 2x. Our read: raise in steps of about 20% every few days, because big jumps reset learning.

2.6 Facebook placements. You opened Facebook on 23 Nov if the experts back it. They do: Sam runs every Meta placement (Facebook, Instagram, Reels, Stories) and cuts one only with clear data, and Hormozi rates Facebook A tier for dads 30 to 45. Our read: all placements from the relaunch, then check the placement breakdown weekly. The placement check (Tue 24 Nov): both past campaigns ran on Instagram only, so we have no Facebook numbers yet. Instagram Feed brought 16 of the 22 leads at $3.39 each, under the $3.96 average. The buyers campaign had no purchases on any placement. Facebook starts from zero at the relaunch, which is one more reason to let Meta test it.

3. Social (25 min)

3.1 Which face carries the brand? You set no faces for now. The brands that grew that way used a character (Duolingo's owl, Duluth's cartoons), real people as recurring ambassadors (YETI), or a show format with no host (Liquid Death). Our read: start with real dad moments told in our voice, and test an animated DOTY character with a few hundred dollars before building it.

3.2 Our first two series. Karten: a series gives people a reason to come back. Candidates: "The minute after" (one Dad Shift moment a week) and 52 Saturday Mornings week by week. Our read: both, starting the first week of December.

3.3 Cadence. Hormozi says volume, up to two a day. Duolingo posts three to five a week, only with a real idea. Our read: one a day on Instagram, and skip a day before posting a weak one.

3.4 Give and ask. Hormozi: give in public, ask in ads and email, and give until they ask. Our read: posts give, the bio and stories ask, and one post in ten names the Dad Score.

3.5 Keyword DMs. ManyChat style: a dad comments a word and gets the free card. Jake says never automate the conversation itself. Our read: automate only the free card; every reply after it is ours.

3.6 Pre scheduling. Hormozi says don't pre schedule. Metricool exists to pre schedule. Our read: schedule the series, post the reactive ones by hand.

7. The app session (60 min, separate)

The full read is in the app and comps brief and the app questions, which also cover the Dec 13 first cut and kids' data and the law: RevenueCat's data on 115,000 subscription apps, Good Inside, Big Little Feelings and the dad apps already out there.

7.1 What's free and where the paid line sits. You set it: most dads will be free, and we learn from the best apps in any category. Duolingo, Strava and Spotify treat free users as their distribution. Our read: a free core good enough to share (the Dad Score, a daily habit, some cards, 52 Saturday Mornings), one clear paid line, and a short reverse trial of premium for new dads, as Strava does.

7.2 Trial length for the paid side. Duolingo cut its trial from 14 to 7 days, and reverse trials lift freemium conversion. Our read: a 7 day reverse trial for new dads.

7.3 Price and plan. Good Inside charges about $23 to $28 a month or $279 a year. The market median in Health and Fitness is about $9.99 a month and $40 a year, and higher priced apps convert better. Annual plans earn about twice what monthly does and keep 28% after a year against 8%. Our read: lead with an annual plan, priced above the median for a deeper product, with monthly as the second option.

7.4 The core promise. Good Inside sells the 9pm "what do I say?" moment to moms. Our read: the dad's version, the minute after he yells, from Dad Shift.

7.5 One habit loop. Duolingo grew daily users 4.5x with streaks, leaderboards and notifications. Our read: one short daily action with a streak, designed with Lauren.

7.6 Who we learn from next. Our read: the best apps in any category (Duolingo, Strava, AllTrails, Headspace and Calm, Spotify, Life360) studied in the product, plus RevenueCat's report and freemium guides, Jorge Mazal's Duolingo growth post and Duolingo's growth model, read in full before the build starts Thursday, then a DOTY app playbook.
