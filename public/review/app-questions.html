1. What the app is for

1.1 The one job. Every great app has one job a user would miss. Duolingo is the daily lesson, Strava is the record of the run, Headspace is the session when you need it. September said the concierge (memory, logistics, the weekend sorted). Since then Dad Shift became the product dads actually buy. Our read: the first job is help in the moment and a better week with your kids; the concierge grows in later. What's the one job in your words?

1.2 Who it's for first. Dads of kids 3 to 14, the same dad as the scorecard. Our read: yes, and only him at launch. Moms and partners join later through sharing.

1.3 How it fits the pillars. Masculinity is good (the dad who leads calmly), brotherhood uplifts (circles of dads), get outside (Saturdays). Our read: all three in the first version, each with one feature, so the app feels like DOTY from day one.

2. Your feature list, sorted

As you write tomorrow's list, mark each item: free or paid, Dec 13 or later. These are the candidates we already have.

From what's built. The Dad Score (and retakes that show progress). Dad Shift's thirteen audio cards. The Phone Talk. 52 Saturday Mornings, one a week, with the photo record.

From September's plan. Onboarding that knows each kid (names, ages, one line on temperament). The family record (a voice line, a photo, a memory). Forward the school email. The bedtime promise. The Thursday digest. The get outside weekend generator. Circles of twelve dads. Booking, teed up then confirmed.

From the sweep. An SOS button for the hard moment, Headspace style, with the Dad Shift moments as the states ("I just yelled," "Screens off meltdown"). A daily reminder set in the first session (Calm's 3x retention). A streak or progress path, Duolingo style. Challenges that post themselves, Strava style.

2.1 What ships Dec 13? Our read on a first cut: the Dad Score, the SOS button with Dad Shift inside, one daily action with a reminder, 52 Saturday Mornings week by week, and onboarding that knows each kid. Circles, the email reader, booking and the full concierge wait.

2.2 What's free forever? Life360 writes a bill of rights for its free tier. Our read: the Dad Score, the SOS button with a few cards, the daily action and 52 Saturday Mornings stay free forever.

2.3 Where's the paid line? RevenueCat: free users should make real progress without reaching the full solution, and higher priced apps convert better. Our read: the full card library and every new pack, the coach that remembers your family, and the circles are paid.

3. Money

3.1 Price. Good Inside: about $23 to $28 a month, $84 a quarter, $279 a year. Dad apps: $5 to $16 a month. Our read: decide between $14.99 a month (September's number) and a Good Inside level price after you see the paid line; our lean is higher, because higher priced apps convert better and the product is deeper than the dad apps.

3.2 Dad Shift buyers. Our read: everyone who bought Dad Shift gets the app's paid tier free for a period, as a thank you and a reverse trial.

3.3 Trial. Our read: a reverse trial of 7 days (premium, then free). Duolingo cut its trial from 14 to 7 days, and one app's reverse trial took freemium conversion from 0.4% to 4.5%.

3.4 Billing. Our read: lead with annual. Annual plans earn about twice what monthly does per install, renew at 83% once active against 39% for monthly, and keep 28% after a year against 8%. Offer monthly too, and a pause in place of cancel. Web billing through Stripe until the store versions.

4. The habit and the first session

4.1 The first session. Headspace lets a new user feel the value before he trusts it. Our read: onboarding asks about his kids, then plays one Dad Shift card for the moment he picks, then asks him to set a daily reminder.

4.2 The daily action. Our read: one 2 to 3 minute thing a day: a card, a question for dinner, or a Saturday plan to pick. Streak counts days he showed up; a missed day never resets his progress to zero (September's rule).

4.2b Reminders are a limited channel. Duolingo set a "protect the channel" rule: more reminders needed strong proof and the CEO's yes. Our read: one reminder a day at most, and you approve any increase.

4.3 Reminders on a web app. On iPhone, push only works after he adds the app to his Home Screen and allows it, about five taps on iOS 26. Our read: walk every dad through Add to Home Screen in onboarding, and run reminders by text and email too until the store versions ship. Do you want texts in the mix?

5. The coach and the AI

5.1 An AI coach at launch? Good Inside's GiGi is their headline feature. Our read: yes, narrow at first: it answers in the DOTY voice from Dad Shift and the four moves, knows his kids' names and ages, and stays out of therapy.

5.2 Safety. Our read: September's minimum before launch: detect self harm, harm to a child and abuse; stop; show 988 and Childhelp; log it. A fixed message, never a generated one.

5.3 Cost. September's ceiling was $3 a member a month. Our read: small models by default, caps on heavy use, free users get a set number of coach questions.

6. Kids' data and the law

6.1 What we store about kids. Our read: first names, ages and his notes only; no photos of kids leave his device without his say; never sold, never used for ads, never used to train models.

6.2 Legal review. Eighteen state chatbot laws were enacted across 2025 and 2026, and California's lets users sue. Our read: a lawyer reviews the privacy policy, terms and the coach before Dec 13. Do you have one, or should we find one?

7. Community

7.1 Circles at launch or later? Strava: the community is the product, and Strava users open the app 35+ times a month. September: circles of twelve dads, no politics, no religion, moderated. Our read: later, after the first 1,000 dads, because an empty room hurts more than no room. Challenges with a shared feed can come first.

7.2 Moderation. Our read: AI first pass plus one human, with the no politics, no religion rule written in.

8. Build and numbers

8.1 Lauren's time. How many hours a week can she give through Dec 13? Our read: the first cut in 2.1 needs her on the onboarding, the home screen and the SOS flow.

8.2 The numbers we watch. Our read: Duolingo's model from day one, every dad in one of seven states each day, and one number a team owns: dads who come back this week after last week. Duolingo found it moved daily users about 5x more than any other lever. Plus activation, free to paid and Dad Score retakes.

8.3 The store versions. Our read: D-U-N-S first, then native apps in January, mainly for push and the App Store's reach.

8.4 The technical calls under the app. These need making before Thursday's build. Our read: dads log in with email and a one tap link (no passwords); web payments run through Stripe, linked to the Dad Shift buyers already there; kids' data is stored encrypted in one place we control, with export and delete on request.
